# Vocabulary data sources and licences

`lexicon.jsonl.gz` is generated by `platform/scripts/vocabulary/build_lexicon.py`. Every entry lists its `sources`;
every sense and example records its own `source`. Nothing in the published lexicon is AI-generated. AI enrichment
(`python -m app.vocabulary.enrich`) stores its output in the database with status `needs_review`. It is never
written back into this file as verified data.

## Quality labels

- `status: curriculum` — at least one sense comes from the curriculum.
- `status: sourced` — meanings come from Wiktionary translation tables. Senses were selected automatically and are
  ranked by how widely each sense is translated. They have **not** been reviewed by a teacher.
- `ipa_status`:
  - `dictionary` — Wiktionary IPA.
  - `dictionary+curriculum` — the curriculum's IPA matches Wiktionary.
  - `composed` — a multi-word phrase built from each word's Wiktionary IPA.
  - `missing` — no reliable pronunciation; no IPA is shown.
  - Curriculum IPA that disagrees with Wiktionary is kept only as `ipa_curriculum` and flagged `ipa_curriculum_differs`.
  - IPA notation is normalised to learner-dictionary style: /r/ for ɹ, /e/ for ɛ, and no tie bars or syllable dots.
- CEFR levels are estimates. Sources, in order: CEFR-J, curriculum section, NGSL frequency, NAWL.
- Topics are assigned heuristically. Evidence, in order: curriculum unit, CEFR-J category, NAWL, Wiktionary topic of the
  most common sense, keyword. The evidence types are stored in `topic_sources`.
