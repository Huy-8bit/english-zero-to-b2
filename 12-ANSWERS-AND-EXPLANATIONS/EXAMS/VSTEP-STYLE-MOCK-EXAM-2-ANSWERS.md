### Part 3

**Talk 1 — Growing up bilingual (Q21–25)**
> Today I'd like to talk about children who grow up speaking two languages and what research suggests about the effects.
>
> For a long time, many parents worried that learning two languages at once would confuse children. In fact, a number of studies suggest that bilingual children may develop certain advantages. One of the most frequently mentioned is the ability to switch between tasks: because bilingual children constantly choose which language to use, they may become better at shifting their attention.
>
> Let me address a common myth. Some people believe that bilingual children fall behind in their language development. It's true that some bilingual children may start speaking slightly later or have a smaller vocabulary in each language at first. However, when both languages are counted together, they usually catch up with their peers within a few years.
>
> There is also some evidence — though researchers are still debating it — that lifelong bilingualism may help to delay the symptoms of dementia in old age.
>
> So, what's my advice for parents? Consistency matters. Some families use the "one parent, one language" approach; others use one language at home and another outside. The exact method is less important than using each language regularly and in a predictable way.

**Talk 2 — Food waste (Q26–30)**
> Food waste is one of those problems that is easy to ignore, yet according to widely cited international estimates, roughly one third of all the food produced in the world is lost or wasted.
>
> Where does this waste happen? In poorer countries, much of it occurs early in the supply chain — on farms or during storage and transport. In richer countries, however, a large share of food is thrown away by households: we buy too much, forget what's in the fridge, or cook more than we can eat.
>
> There are some encouraging solutions. In many cities, apps now allow restaurants and bakeries to sell food that hasn't been sold by the end of the day at a much lower price, instead of throwing it away.
>
> One simple problem is confusion over food labels. Many people don't understand the difference between "best before" and "use by" dates. "Use by" is about safety, but "best before" is about quality — food is often perfectly safe to eat after its "best before" date.
>
> My practical advice is this: plan your meals for the week and write a shopping list before you go to the supermarket. It sounds obvious, but it's one of the most effective ways to reduce waste — and to save money.

**Talk 3 — Why we procrastinate (Q31–35)**
> Most of us procrastinate from time to time — we put off tasks we know we should be doing. Many people assume this is a problem of time management or simple laziness. However, many psychologists now argue that procrastination is mainly about managing emotions.
>
> Think about writing a difficult report. The task might make us feel anxious, bored or afraid of failure. Procrastinating gives us a quick escape from those uncomfortable feelings — even though it creates more stress later.
>
> So, what can we do? The first strategy is to break the task into small, specific steps. "Write the report" feels overwhelming; "write three bullet points for the introduction" feels manageable.
>
> A second strategy is what some people call the "two-minute start". You simply commit to working on the task for just two minutes. Often, once you've started, it's much easier to continue.
>
> Finally, a word about self-criticism. Many people respond to procrastination by being very hard on themselves. But research suggests that this often makes the problem worse, because it increases the negative emotions we were trying to avoid. Being a little kinder to yourself may actually help you get started.

## WRITING

Dùng rubric /10 cho mỗi task trong [Mock Exam 1 Answers](VSTEP-STYLE-MOCK-EXAM-1-ANSWERS.md#writing).

### Task 1 — Bài mẫu (~170 từ)

> *Dear Sir or Madam,*
>
> *I am writing to complain about the construction work on the new apartment block on Hoa Binh Street, which has been causing serious problems for local residents for the past three months.*
>
> *Firstly, work frequently begins before 6 a.m. and continues after 10 p.m., often with loud drilling and heavy machinery. Secondly, large trucks regularly block the street, making it difficult for residents to leave their homes and for emergency vehicles to pass.*
>
> *These problems are having a significant impact on our daily lives. Many residents, including elderly people and young children, are unable to sleep properly, and several neighbours have had to take time off work because of exhaustion. Parents also worry about their children's safety when walking to school past the trucks.*
>
> *I would therefore be grateful if the council could limit construction hours to between 7 a.m. and 6 p.m. and require the company to arrange proper parking for its vehicles. Regular inspections would also help to ensure that these rules are followed.*
>
> *I look forward to your reply.*
>
> *Yours faithfully,*
> *Tran Thi Lan*

### Task 2 — Bài mẫu (~280 từ)

> *In the age of social media, celebrities have more influence than ever, especially over young people who follow their lives online every day. This has led to a debate about whether famous people have a duty to behave as role models. In my view, they do have some responsibility, although their private lives should not be judged too harshly.*
>
> *On the one hand, celebrities choose to live in the public eye and benefit greatly from their fame. Many earn huge amounts of money from advertising, precisely because young fans want to copy them. It therefore seems reasonable to expect them to avoid behaviour that could harm their followers, such as promoting dangerous diets or drink-driving. When a famous footballer speaks out against racism, or a singer encourages fans to volunteer, the positive impact can be enormous.*
>
> *On the other hand, celebrities are human beings, and it is unrealistic to expect them to be perfect. Singers and athletes are usually famous for their talent, not for their moral qualities, and they did not necessarily choose to become role models. Moreover, constant media attention can put enormous pressure on them, sometimes leading to serious mental health problems.*
>
> *On balance, I believe that celebrities should act responsibly in public, particularly on social media, where their words reach millions of young people. However, their private mistakes should not be the main focus of attention. Parents, teachers and communities have a far greater influence on young people's values than any celebrity, and it is their example that matters most.*
>
> *In conclusion, while celebrities cannot be expected to be perfect, they should recognise the influence they have and use it wisely.*

*I think taking lessons with an online tutor twice a week is the best option for my friend. Since he's busy with work, he needs something flexible, and online lessons can be scheduled in the evening or during his lunch break. More importantly, a tutor can correct his mistakes and focus on his specific weaknesses, which is essential for improving speaking.
