> ⚠️ Đây là **đề mô phỏng định dạng**, không phải đề VSTEP chính thức. Đáp án và rubric dưới đây do giáo trình biên soạn để **luyện tập và tự đánh giá**; **không** dùng để quy đổi ra bậc VSTEP hay điểm chính thức. Rubric Viết/Nói được thiết kế theo các tiêu chí đánh giá phổ biến (hoàn thành nhiệm vụ, tổ chức, từ vựng, ngữ pháp, phát âm/độ trôi chảy), không sao chép thang chấm của bất kỳ đơn vị tổ chức thi nào.

## Cách tự chấm

- **Listening** /35 và **Reading** /40: mỗi câu đúng 1 điểm. Ghi lại số câu sai theo **dạng câu hỏi** (ý chính, chi tiết, suy luận, từ vựng, quy chiếu) để biết cần luyện gì.
- **Writing:** Task 1 /10, Task 2 /10 theo rubric; Task 2 quan trọng hơn (dành nhiều thời gian hơn).
- **Speaking:** /10 theo rubric.
- Mốc tự học gợi ý (nội bộ, không chính thức): Listening ≥ 21/35, Reading ≥ 24/40, mỗi bài viết ≥ 6/10, Speaking ≥ 6/10 trong điều kiện thi mô phỏng.

### Part 3

**Talk 1 — Sleep and memory (Q21–25)**
> Today I want to talk about something every student does but rarely thinks about: sleep — and in particular, how sleep affects learning.
>
> For a long time, people believed that the brain simply "switched off" at night. We now know that this is far from true. During deep sleep, the brain appears to replay the experiences of the day, strengthening important memories and organising them so that they can be retrieved later.
>
> In one well-known type of experiment, students learn a list of new words in the evening. Half of them then sleep normally, while the other half stay awake. When they are tested the next day, the students who slept usually remember significantly more words.
>
> This has important implications for exam preparation. Many students stay up all night before an exam, hoping to cram in as much information as possible. Unfortunately, this is often counterproductive: without sleep, the brain has less opportunity to consolidate what has been learned, and tiredness reduces concentration during the exam itself.
>
> So, what should you do? My advice is simple: review the most important material in the evening, shortly before you go to bed, and then get a full night's sleep. You may be surprised by how much you remember in the morning.

**Talk 2 — Urban beekeeping (Q26–30)**
> In recent years, an unusual hobby has been growing in cities around the world: beekeeping. Concerned by reports that bee populations are declining, many city residents want to do something to help, and keeping a hive seems like a practical way to contribute.
>
> Since space on the ground is limited, many urban beekeepers keep their hives on rooftops — of apartment blocks, offices and even hotels. The bees fly down to parks, gardens and balconies to collect nectar.
>
> Surprisingly, city bees can be very productive. Because cities contain such a wide variety of plants — in parks, gardens and along streets — urban honey often has a more varied flavour than honey from farms where only one crop is grown.
>
> However, some scientists have raised a concern. If too many hives are placed in a small area, there may not be enough flowers to feed all the bees. This could create competition not only among honeybees but also with wild bees and other insects, which may actually be harmed.
>
> So, if you'd like to help bees, you don't necessarily need to start a hive. One of the most effective things you can do is simply plant flowers — especially local species — on your balcony or in your garden.

**Talk 3 — The psychology of habits (Q31–35)**
> How much of what you do every day is actually a decision? According to some researchers, a large proportion of our daily actions are not conscious choices at all, but habits.
>
> Psychologists often describe habits as a loop with three parts. First, there is a cue — something that triggers the behaviour, such as a time of day or a feeling. Then comes the routine — the behaviour itself. Finally, there is a reward, which makes the brain want to repeat the loop.
>
> This explains why so many New Year's resolutions fail. People decide to change, but they rely only on willpower, without changing the cues and rewards around them. Willpower, unfortunately, tends to run out.
>
> A more effective approach is to keep the cue and the reward, but change the routine. For example, if you usually eat snacks in the evening because you feel bored, you might replace the snack with a short walk, which also relieves boredom.
>
> My main piece of advice is this: start small. If you want to start exercising, begin with two minutes a day. It sounds almost too easy, but tiny habits are much more likely to last — and once they're established, you can gradually build on them.

## WRITING

### Rubric (mỗi task /10)

| Tiêu chí | 0 | 1 | 2 | 2,5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hoàn thành nhiệm vụ** | Lạc đề / quá ngắn | Thiếu ý, phát triển yếu | Đủ ý, phát triển tương đối | Đủ ý, phát triển tốt, đúng độ dài |
| **Tổ chức & liên kết** | Rời rạc | Có đoạn, liên kết máy móc | Mạch lạc, liên kết đa dạng | Mạch lạc tự nhiên, quy chiếu tốt |
| **Từ vựng** | Rất hạn chế | Lặp, sai collocation | Đa dạng vừa đủ, ít lỗi | Đa dạng, chính xác, văn phong phù hợp |
| **Ngữ pháp** | Lỗi gây khó hiểu | Câu đơn giản, nhiều lỗi | Có câu phức, lỗi không cản trở | Đa dạng, chính xác cao |

> Tổng tối đa 10/task. Bài dưới số từ tối thiểu bị giới hạn tối đa 1 điểm ở tiêu chí "Hoàn thành nhiệm vụ".

### Task 1 — Bài mẫu (~150 từ)

> *Dear Alex,*
>
> *I've just arrived home, and I wanted to write straight away to thank you for such a wonderful week. You and your family made me feel completely at home, and I really appreciate the time you spent showing me around.*
>
> *What I enjoyed most was the day we cycled along the river and had a picnic in the park. I'd never seen so many people out enjoying the sunshine! I also loved trying your mum's homemade pie — please tell her I'm still dreaming about it.*
>
> *Now it's your turn to visit me! How about coming in December, when the weather in Hanoi is cool and pleasant? We could explore the Old Quarter, take a boat trip in Ninh Binh, and of course I'll introduce you to real Vietnamese street food.*
>
> *Let me know what you think.*
>
> *Best wishes,*
> *Nam*

### Task 2 — Bài mẫu (~280 từ)

> *Foreign languages are a compulsory subject in many school systems, including Vietnam's. However, there is an ongoing debate about whether students should be required to study them or be free to choose. Both sides present reasonable arguments.*
>
> *Supporters of compulsory language learning argue that it offers long-term benefits that young students may not yet appreciate. In a globalised economy, knowledge of a foreign language — especially English — can open doors to better jobs, higher education and international travel. Moreover, learning a language is widely believed to develop cognitive skills such as memory and problem-solving, as well as an understanding of other cultures. If the subject were optional, many students might avoid it simply because it seems difficult, only to regret this decision later.*
>
> *On the other hand, those who favour choice point out that not every student has the same interests or abilities. Forcing a reluctant learner to study a language for many years may lead to frustration and poor results. They argue that the time could be better spent on subjects that match students' strengths, such as science, art or vocational training. Furthermore, motivated learners usually make much faster progress than those who are simply following rules.*
>
> *In my opinion, a foreign language should be compulsory in primary and lower secondary school, but students should have more choice afterwards. Early exposure gives every child the opportunity to discover whether they enjoy languages, while later freedom allows older students to focus on their goals. Admittedly, this approach requires flexible timetables. Nevertheless, it balances the long-term benefits of language learning with respect for individual differences.*
>
> *In conclusion, although both views have merit, a combination of compulsory early learning and later choice seems the most sensible solution.*

### Part 2 — Gợi ý trả lời

*In my opinion, allowing employees to work from home two days a week is the best option. The main reason is that commuting is one of the biggest sources of stress in big cities — in Hanoi, many people spend over an hour in traffic every day. Working from home would give them more time to rest and to be with their families. Yoga classes are a nice idea, but not everyone enjoys yoga, and a one-hour class once a week may not make a big difference. As for an extra week of holiday, it would certainly be welcome, but its effect would only last a short time; employees would still face daily stress for the other fifty-one weeks.*

### Part 3 — Câu hỏi tiếp nối (người hỗ trợ dùng)

1. *Do you think online learning will replace traditional classrooms in the future?*
2. *What are the disadvantages of using technology in education?*
3. *How can teachers help students use the internet responsibly?*

**Gợi ý phát triển ý:** *Online courses* → học mọi lúc, mọi nơi, chi phí thấp (ví dụ: khóa học trực tuyến miễn phí của các đại học); *Interactive tools* → ứng dụng, trò chơi giúp học sinh hứng thú hơn; *Access to information* → tra cứu nhanh, nhưng cần kỹ năng đánh giá nguồn; *Ý riêng* → giáo viên dùng dữ liệu để theo dõi tiến độ từng học sinh.
