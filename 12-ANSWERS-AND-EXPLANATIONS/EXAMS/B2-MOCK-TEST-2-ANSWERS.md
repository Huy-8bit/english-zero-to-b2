## D — Listening (15 điểm)

### Recording 1 — Chỉ đưa đoạn dưới vào TTS

> Hi, and welcome back to *Simple Living*. Today I want to talk about how I became a minimalist. It started about five years ago, when I moved from a big house into a tiny flat in the city centre. I had to decide what to keep, and I realised I hadn't used most of my things in years. In the end, I sold or gave away about half of everything I owned.
>
> The biggest change has been financial. Because I think carefully before buying anything, I now spend much less on clothes and gadgets, and I've managed to save enough for a trip to Japan next year.
>
> I won't pretend it's always easy. The hardest part, honestly, is gifts. My family loves giving presents, and I've had to explain — very politely — that I'd rather they spent time with me than money on me.
>
> One thing I'd like to clear up: many people think minimalism means owning almost nothing and living in an empty white room. It doesn't. It simply means keeping the things that are useful or meaningful to you, and letting go of the rest.
>
> If you're thinking of starting, my advice is: don't try to do everything in one weekend. Start with one drawer, or one shelf. Small steps are much more likely to last.

### Recording 2 — Chỉ đưa hội thoại dưới vào TTS

> **Adviser:** Hi, come in. How can I help?
> **Student:** Hi. I'm interested in the exchange programme with Melbourne. I saw a poster about it.
> **Adviser:** Ah, the Pacific Partners exchange programme. Yes, it's very popular. It lasts six months, usually from February to July.
> **Student:** What are the requirements?
> **Adviser:** You need a minimum grade point average of 3.2, and you must have completed at least two years of your degree.
> **Student:** I've got a 3.4, so that should be fine. When's the deadline?
> **Adviser:** The 30th of September. Along with your transcript, you'll need to submit a personal statement explaining why you want to go.
> **Student:** OK. My main worry, to be honest, is money. Living costs in Australia are much higher than here, and my family can't support me.
> **Adviser:** I understand. The good news is that tuition is covered, because it's an exchange. And there's a travel grant you can apply for at the same time. Many students also work part-time while they're there — your visa allows that.
> **Student:** That's really helpful. So, what should I do now?
> **Adviser:** I'd suggest that you attend the information session next Wednesday. You can talk to students who went last year.
> **Student:** Great. I'll sign up for that today and start drafting my personal statement this weekend.

## E — Writing (20 điểm)

### Rubric E1 — Article (/8)

| Tiêu chí | 2 điểm |
|---|---|
| Nội dung | Tiêu đề, giới thiệu kỹ năng, ≥ 2 lý do, cách học, kết bài |
| Thu hút người đọc | Câu hỏi tu từ, giọng văn sinh động, nói trực tiếp với người đọc |
| Tổ chức | Đoạn rõ, liên kết tốt |
| Ngôn ngữ | Cấu trúc B2 đa dạng, chính xác |

### Mẫu E1 (~195 từ)

> ***Forget algebra — learn to manage your money!***
>
> *How many of your friends know what a budget is? How many could explain the difference between saving and investing? If the answer is "not many", you're not alone. Yet managing money may be the most useful skill you never learned at school.*
>
> *Firstly, every adult has to deal with money, whatever their career. Paying rent, avoiding debt and planning for emergencies are challenges we all face. Had I learned about budgeting earlier, I would have avoided some expensive mistakes during my first year at university.*
>
> *Secondly, financial skills give you freedom. People who save regularly are able to take opportunities — a course abroad, a new business, a career change — that others simply cannot afford.*
>
> *So how can you learn? Start small. Track every expense for a month using a free app. Read one article a week about personal finance. Better still, ask your parents how they manage the household budget; you may be surprised by what they know.*
>
> *Money might not buy happiness, but understanding it can certainly prevent a lot of unhappiness. It's never too early to start.*

### Mẫu E2 (~265 từ)

> *Thanks to advances in medicine, nutrition and living conditions, life expectancy has risen dramatically in many parts of the world. While this is clearly a remarkable achievement, it also brings significant challenges. In my view, however, the advantages outweigh the disadvantages.*
>
> *The most obvious benefit is that people have more time to enjoy life. Many older people remain healthy well into their seventies and eighties, which allows them to travel, pursue hobbies and spend time with their grandchildren. Furthermore, older people possess valuable experience. In families, grandparents often provide childcare, enabling parents to work; in workplaces, experienced employees can mentor younger colleagues.*
>
> *Admittedly, longer lives create economic pressures. As the proportion of elderly people increases, governments must spend more on pensions and healthcare, while the working-age population that pays taxes may shrink. In countries such as Japan, these issues have already become a major political concern. There is also a risk that many people will spend their final years in poor health or loneliness.*
>
> *Nevertheless, these problems are not impossible to solve. Raising the retirement age gradually, encouraging healthy lifestyles and developing community support for the elderly could reduce many of the costs. Moreover, if older people are able to keep working part-time, they can continue to contribute to the economy.*
>
> *In conclusion, although an ageing population presents real challenges, I believe that the opportunity to live longer, healthier lives is a benefit that societies should welcome — provided that they plan carefully for its consequences.*

## F — Speaking (15 điểm)

Câu hỏi tiếp nối gợi ý cho người hỗ trợ (Part 3):
- *What are the main reasons why towns change?* → *Is change always positive?*
- *Should historic buildings always be protected?* → *Who should pay for restoration?*
- *How might Vietnamese cities look in 30 years?* → *What would you like to stay the same?*

Mẫu F2 (trích): *The place I'd like to describe is the area around my primary school in Bien Hoa. When I was a child, it was surrounded by rice fields, and we used to catch fish in the canals after school. Now the fields have been replaced by apartment blocks and a shopping centre. On the one hand, life is more convenient — there's a hospital nearby. On the other hand, I miss the open space. If those fields still existed, children today would be able to play outside much more.*
