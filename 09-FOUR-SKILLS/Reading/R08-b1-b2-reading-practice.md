## Text 1 (B1) — The Rise of Remote Learning

> **(1)** A few years ago, online courses were seen as a poor substitute for "real" education. Today, millions of people around the world study online, from schoolchildren to retired professionals.
>
> **(2)** The most obvious advantage is flexibility. Learners can study at any time and from any place with an internet connection. For working adults, this makes it possible to gain new qualifications without giving up their jobs. Online courses are also often cheaper than traditional ones, as there are no costs for buildings or travel.
>
> **(3)** However, online learning is not suitable for everyone. It requires a high level of self-discipline, and some learners find it hard to stay motivated without a teacher in the room. Others miss the social side of education — chatting with classmates, working in groups and asking questions face to face.
>
> **(4)** Technology itself can also be a barrier. Not everyone has a reliable internet connection or a suitable device, particularly in rural areas. This means that online education can sometimes increase, rather than reduce, inequality.
>
> **(5)** Many experts now believe that the future lies in "blended learning", which combines online study with some classroom time. This approach aims to offer the flexibility of online courses together with the support and interaction of traditional teaching.

**1.** What is the main idea of paragraph 1?
A. Retired people prefer classroom learning. B. Only young people study online. C. Online courses are poor quality. D. Attitudes to online learning have changed.

**2.** According to paragraph 2, why are online courses often cheaper?
A. They are shorter. B. Governments pay for them. C. Teachers are paid less. D. There are no building or travel costs.

**3.** The word *barrier* in paragraph 4 is closest in meaning to:
A. advantage B. tool C. obstacle D. solution

**4.** The word *This* in paragraph 4 refers to:
A. the fact that some people lack internet access or devices B. rural areas C. online education D. technology

**5.** True / False / Not Given: Online learners always have lower motivation than classroom learners.

**6.** What is "blended learning"?

**7.** What is the writer's attitude towards online learning?
A. Uninterested B. Completely positive C. Balanced D. Completely negative

## Text 2 (B2) — Is Tourism Loving Places to Death?

> **(1)** Few industries have grown as rapidly as international tourism. Cheap flights, online booking and social media have made it easier than ever to travel, and destinations that were once known only to a few adventurous visitors now receive millions of tourists a year.
>
> **(2)** The economic benefits are undeniable. Tourism creates jobs, supports local businesses and brings foreign currency into developing economies. In some regions, it is the main source of income for entire communities.
>
> **(3)** Yet the costs are becoming harder to ignore. In popular destinations, residents complain of overcrowded streets, rising rents and the loss of local character as shops serving residents are replaced by souvenir stores. Fragile natural sites, such as coral reefs and mountain trails, can suffer serious damage when visitor numbers exceed what they can sustain.
>
> **(4)** Some governments have begun to respond. Measures include limiting the number of daily visitors to particular sites, introducing tourist taxes and restricting short-term holiday rentals. Critics of such policies argue that they make travel more expensive and could harm small businesses that depend on tourists. Supporters counter that without regulation, the very attractions that draw visitors may be destroyed.
>
> **(5)** Ultimately, the challenge is not to stop tourism but to manage it. Spreading visitors across more destinations and seasons, encouraging longer stays rather than brief visits, and involving local communities in decision-making are all approaches that experts frequently recommend. Whether they succeed may determine whether today's most beautiful places remain beautiful for future generations.

**8.** Which factor is NOT mentioned in paragraph 1 as a reason for the growth of tourism?
A. Higher salaries B. Social media C. Cheap flights D. Online booking

**9.** The word *undeniable* in paragraph 2 is closest in meaning to:
A. temporary B. clearly true C. uncertain D. small

**10.** According to paragraph 3, why do local shops disappear?
A. Rents fall. B. They are replaced by souvenir shops. C. Residents move away. D. The government closes them.

**11.** What can be inferred from the phrase *exceed what they can sustain* (paragraph 3)?
A. Natural sites can handle unlimited visitors. B. There is a limit to how many visitors natural sites can cope with. C. Natural sites are expensive to visit. D. Tourists prefer natural sites.

**12.** In paragraph 4, what do critics of the new measures fear?
A. Attractions will be destroyed. B. Travel will cost more and small businesses may suffer. C. More tourists will come. D. Taxes will be too low.

**13.** What is the main idea of paragraph 5?
A. Tourism should be banned. B. Only local people should travel. C. Tourism needs to be managed well. D. Tourists should stay at home.

**14.** Choose the best title for Text 2:
A. How to Book Cheap Flights B. Balancing the Benefits and Costs of Tourism C. The Economic Benefits of Tourism D. The History of Coral Reefs

**15.** Viết 2 câu tóm tắt Text 2 bằng lời của bạn (≤ 40 từ).
